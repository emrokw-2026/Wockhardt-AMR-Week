01 / Why this matters

AMR needs more than awareness.

It needs a clearer view of clinical realities, continued scientific innovation and better-informed decisions across the care pathway. THE AMR PULSE brings these threads into one conversation—grounded in the experience of doctors across India.

03 — INFORM

Translate insight into priorities

Use reviewed findings to support constructive dialogue on research, diagnostics, treatment and stewardship.

02 / Our approach

Innovation and stewardship belong in the same conversation.

The question is not simply “How late can we use it?”
It is “What is right for this patient, now?”

Treatment decisions remain the responsibility of qualified clinicians and must follow approved indications, local guidance and patient-specific assessment.

↗

Keep antibiotic innovation moving

Scientific discovery and development are essential parts of the long-term response to resistant infections. New options must be supported by robust evidence and responsible use.

◎

Make stewardship precise, not simplistic

Stewardship is not merely delaying newer antibiotics. It means selecting appropriate therapy for the individual patient, informed by severity, likely pathogens, microbiology, susceptibility, current guidance and reassessment.

⌁

Connect the ecosystem

Doctors, microbiologists, researchers, pharmacists, health systems, payers and public-health stakeholders all have a role in the AMR response.

✓

Let evidence lead

Campaign insights should be reported with clear methods and limitations. Clinical claims should be verifiable, balanced and reviewed before publication.

05 / Wockhardt | Research with Purpose

Committed to the science—and the shared responsibility.

Wockhardt’s AMR engagement is an opportunity to contribute to a wider conversation: supporting antibiotic research, listening to clinical needs and encouraging evidence-led, responsible decisions. Credibility must be earned through transparency and action.
